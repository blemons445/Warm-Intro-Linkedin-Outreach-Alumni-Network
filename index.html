Questions

Is it free?

Yes. Try it on this site with no account, or run the skill in your own Claude account, which has a free plan.

Does it send messages for me?

No. It drafts them and you review and send each one yourself on LinkedIn.

Do I need an account?

No. Fill in three fields on Try it and the site does the searching. Running the skill in Claude with Clay connected finds more alumni if you want to go deeper.

Where is my data stored?

Your tracker lives only in your own browser. When you use Try it, your answers are sent to Claude to run the search and aren't saved by this site. Use Back up on the Tracker page to keep a copy.

Does it work for any school?

Yes. It was built at Texas A&M, but you tell it your school and it searches for your alumni.

      How it was built

      I built Warm Intro during my own Summer 2027 internship search as a junior marketing major at Texas A&M. Every rule in it came from something that went wrong or right in real use.

      The problem

      Applying online feels like sending resumes into a void. Referrals are a small share of applications but a large share of hires, yet most students don't know who to message or what to say. I wanted a tool that does the research and leaves the conversations to me.

      How it works

      Decisions that came from testing

      • Check postings on the company's own site. Job boards keep closed and last-year listings up. A posting only counts if its application page loads and its title matches the right term.
      • Identify companies by LinkedIn URL, not web domain. In testing, searching by domain matched the wrong company: pga.org returned a small fund in Florida and topgolf.com returned Topgolf Abu Dhabi.
      • Confirm every alumnus one by one. Clay's school filter matched loosely and returned people from other schools. Similar names don't count either: Texas A&M-Commerce is not Texas A&M. About 1 in 9 current marketing and recruiting staff at a target company turned out to be alumni.
      • Prefer recent graduates. People 1 to 4 years out remember the internship search and are more likely to reply.
      • Ask one easy question, not for a call. The first message asks something a person can answer in a reply. A 15-minute chat comes only after they respond.
      • Apply first, then message the same day. Programs review applications as they arrive, so waiting on a referral is the bigger risk.
      • Be honest about tools. I tested Apollo as a second people source and found its people search needs a paid plan, so the setup now says so instead of promising it's free.

      Results so far

      On my own search: 10 open Dallas-Fort Worth internships found and verified, 3 applications, 6 LinkedIn messages sent and 1 reply, all logged in the tracker.
